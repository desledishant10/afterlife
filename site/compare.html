Comparison

Afterlife vs Prowler and Cartography

These three get named in the same breath, but they answer different questions. This is an honest map of which tool fits which question, including the ones where Afterlife is not the answer.

Three tools, three different questions

Before the feature grid, the one distinction that actually decides which to use: what question each was built to answer.

Prowler

also ScoutSuite · Apache-2.0 / open source

Is this cloud account configured to a benchmark?

Config posture and compliance for one cloud at a time. Hundreds of checks mapped to CIS, PCI, HIPAA, SOC 2, and more. If you need a CIS benchmark across your AWS accounts, this is the tool, not Afterlife.

Cartography

Apache-2.0 / open source · Neo4j

Show me all my infrastructure as a graph I can query.

An asset-inventory graph across many sources, including AWS, GCP, GitHub, Okta, and Google. It hands you the nodes, the edges, and a Neo4j to run Cypher against. Enormous flexibility; you write the analysis.

Afterlife

MIT core · self-hosted, no graph DB

Whose access outlived them, across systems, and which hurts most?

Collapses a person's accounts into one identity, runs 16 ghost-access rules over that join, and ranks findings by blast radius. Out of the box, no graph database, nothing to query.

When to reach for each

Prowler or ScoutSuite

reach for it when

You need CIS or compliance-framework benchmarking, deep single-cloud config coverage, or an auditor-shaped posture report for one cloud. This is a lane Afterlife does not compete in.

Cartography

reach for it when

You want the raw relationship graph to interrogate yourself, you are comfortable running Neo4j and writing Cypher, and you would rather have total flexibility than opinionated rules someone else wrote.

Afterlife

reach for it when

Your risk is standing access that outlived people across cloud, code, and IdP; you want the offboarded-owner join made and ranked for you; and you want it self-hosted with nothing to query or stand up.

They stack, they do not collide

This is not a "rip out your existing tools" pitch. Afterlife reads the same read-only APIs and answers a question the others leave on the table, so it layers on top of whatever you already run.

A realistic pipeline for a team that already has some of this:

  • 01Prowler for CIS and compliance benchmarks on each cloud, on the schedule your auditor expects.
  • 02Cartography if you want a queryable inventory graph for ad-hoc investigation.
  • 03Afterlife for the cross-source ghost-access layer none of them rank for you: who is still live after they left, and which of those opens the most doors.
